# Idea engine

Use this reference when the user wants ideas, improvements, campaigns, new commercial features or something “revolutionary.”

## Step 1 — Frame the job

Write one sentence:

> We want [specific audience] to [specific behavior] because [business objective], while preserving [important constraint].

Examples:

- We want occasional buyers to make a second purchase within 30 days while protecting margin.
- We want resellers to route more monthly volume through the store without publicly exposing internal discount logic.
- We want new visitors to trust the store enough to complete a first purchase on mobile.

## Step 2 — Find the tension

Good marketing ideas resolve a tension.

Look for:

- customer wants speed but fears fraud
- customer wants lower price but also reliability
- customer wants variety but gets decision fatigue
- reseller wants better economics but needs predictable service
- customer wants rewards but does not want complicated points
- user wants convenience but does not want invasive personalization

State the tension before brainstorming.

## Step 3 — Generate across six levers

Generate candidates from different mechanisms so the list is not repetitive.

### A. Offer

- bundle
- threshold reward
- cross-sell
- guarantee/reassurance if operationally supportable
- personalized reward
- bonus value rather than raw discount

### B. Experience

- reduce steps
- saved preferences
- smarter recommendations
- one-tap repeat purchase
- clearer status/progress
- proactive reassurance

### C. Distribution

- referral loop
- reseller loop
- creator/community distribution
- partnerships
- shareable artifact/result
- social-to-store flow

### D. Status / progression

- tiers
- streaks only when natural
- milestones
- unlocks
- early access
- reseller achievement/status

### E. Trust

- transparent process
- verified proof
- clearer fulfillment expectations
- visible support path
- independent validation
- post-purchase follow-up

### F. Category / positioning

- reframe the offer around the job customers actually hire it for
- create a useful bundle/category competitors do not frame well
- emphasize a real strength competitors bury
- simplify a confusing market choice

## Step 4 — Force non-obvious thinking

For at least one candidate, ask each question:

- What would we do if paid ads disappeared tomorrow?
- What would make a customer voluntarily tell another person?
- What could make the buying process itself memorable?
- What can we personalize from first-party behavior without being intrusive?
- What asset could compound over time instead of being consumed once?
- What would make a reseller more successful, not merely cheaper?
- Which customer anxiety is competitors ignoring?
- What could we remove rather than add?
- What is currently invisible value that could be made visible?

## Step 5 — Build three bands

Always include, when appropriate:

### Core improvement

Low novelty, strong expected value, easy to test.

### Adjacent advantage

Uses an existing asset/capability in a new way.

### Asymmetric bet

More unusual and differentiated, but testable with a small downside.

Do not recommend the asymmetric bet merely because it sounds exciting.

## Step 6 — Score ideas

Use a 1–5 scale when ranking helps.

| Factor | Question |
| --- | --- |
| Customer value | Does the user clearly benefit? |
| Differentiation | Is this hard to confuse with everyone else? |
| Revenue/retention | Can it plausibly affect meaningful behavior? |
| Evidence/confidence | Do we have reasons to believe it? |
| Speed to learn | Can we validate quickly? |
| Effort | How much product/ops work? |
| Margin risk | Can it destroy unit economics? |
| Operational risk | Can support/fulfillment handle it? |
| Brand fit | Does it strengthen the desired perception? |

Favor high customer value + fast learning + manageable downside.

## Step 7 — Design the smallest useful test

Do not fully build a concept just to find out nobody wants it.

Possible tests:

- manual concierge version
- one-segment offer
- landing-page message test
- banner/CTA test
- limited customer cohort
- reseller pilot
- post-purchase message
- waitlist/interest capture
- simple bundle assembled from existing products
- customer interview/survey when behavior cannot be observed yet

Never use a deceptive fake checkout or fake availability to validate demand.

## Useful ideation patterns for a digital-commerce store

These are patterns, not automatic recommendations.

### Personal-value ladder

Instead of giving everyone the same discount, increase value based on behavior that helps both customer and business: repeat purchase, category breadth, referrals, reseller volume, verified feedback or wallet usage.

### Discovery-to-proof loop

Content/creator introduces the service → customer sees independent/verified proof → store resolves uncertainty → purchase → satisfied customer generates new proof/referral.

### Reseller success loop

Reseller gets easier operations or better economics → sells more reliably → routes more volume → unlocks better tools/value → becomes more dependent on service quality rather than merely price.

### Repeat-purchase shortcut

After a successful transaction, make the next relevant purchase substantially easier using saved context, reminders or personalized shortcuts with user control.

### Cross-category progression

Reward exploration of genuinely related categories rather than arbitrary spending. This can increase breadth while teaching customers what else the store solves.

### Surprise-and-delight budget

Instead of permanently lowering price, reserve a controlled budget for occasional high-salience rewards, personalized bonuses or status moments. Measure whether the surprise creates repeat behavior or word of mouth.

## Red flags

Reject or rework ideas that depend on:

- permanent discounts with no margin model
- fake scarcity
- fabricated testimonials
- confusing price anchoring
- spammy notifications
- an expensive build before validation
- copying a competitor without a strategic reason
- “AI” as a feature with no customer benefit
- loyalty points with no meaningful redemption value
- referrals where the reward exceeds plausible customer value
- too many mechanics competing for attention

## Final recommendation format

When possible, end ideation with:

**Best first move:** one idea.

**Why:** 2–4 sentences.

**MVP:** smallest test.

**Metric:** primary success metric + one or two guardrails.

**If it works:** next scale step.

**If it fails:** what was learned and what to test next.
