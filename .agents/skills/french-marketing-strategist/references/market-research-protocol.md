# Market research protocol

Use this when the task depends on what is true now rather than evergreen strategy.

## Define the market before searching

Write down:

- Category/product/service
- Geography
- Customer segment
- Time window
- Commercial question

Examples:

- “Game top-up offers for consumers in Bolivia in the last 90 days”
- “Current streaming-service reseller alternatives in Bolivia/LatAm”
- “2026 loyalty patterns for ecommerce with frequent low-ticket purchases”

Without this frame, research tends to collect interesting but irrelevant facts.

## Evidence stack

Prefer:

1. Official product/company/platform pages for current product facts, terms and prices.
2. Government/regulator/institutional data for legal, economic or market facts.
3. Recent high-quality industry research for broader patterns.
4. Trade press/interviews for interpretation.
5. Customer reviews, forums, comments and social posts for pain-point language and qualitative signals.
6. Secondary summaries only when better evidence is unavailable.

Never use a single anecdote as proof of a market-wide behavior.

## Competitor scan

For each relevant competitor or alternative, capture only what matters:

- Target audience
- Main promise
- Offer structure
- Price/packaging if public and current
- Trust mechanisms
- Convenience/friction
- Channel/distribution
- Loyalty/referral mechanics
- Differentiation
- Weakness/opportunity

Include non-obvious alternatives. A buyer can solve the same job through a direct competitor, marketplace, informal seller, bank/app promotion, direct platform purchase, or by doing nothing.

## Trend validation

Before calling something a trend, check:

- Is the source recent enough for the category?
- Is it reporting actual behavior, marketer intention, or merely predictions?
- Is it global, US-only, LatAm, or local?
- Does the target audience resemble The French Store's customers?
- Is the trend big enough to matter or just interesting?

Label each conclusion as one of:

- **Observed:** directly supported by current evidence.
- **Likely:** supported by multiple adjacent signals but not directly measured for this market.
- **Hypothesis:** plausible idea that still needs validation.

## Current 2026 watchlist

These are research prompts, not conclusions that must be recommended.

### AI discovery and conversational commerce

Shopify's 2026 material describes AI-assisted product discovery, personalization and conversational/agentic commerce as increasingly relevant ecommerce surfaces.

Useful sources:

- https://www.shopify.com/blog/ecommerce-trends-predictions
- https://www.shopify.com/blog/ai-ecommerce
- https://www.shopify.com/enterprise/blog/social-commerce-trends

Questions to validate locally:

- Are customers using AI tools to compare or discover these products/services?
- Is product information structured clearly enough for search/AI discovery?
- Would conversational support reduce purchase uncertainty?

### Personalization and loyalty

Recent Shopify 2026 research highlights more individualized rewards and first-party data-driven experiences.

Useful sources:

- https://www.shopify.com/blog/ai-personalization-ecommerce
- https://www.shopify.com/blog/ai-personalization-marketing

Questions:

- What first-party behavior do we already have permission to use?
- Can we personalize recommendations/rewards without becoming creepy or invasive?
- Does personalization create meaningful value or just cosmetic variation?

### Social commerce, creators and proof

Recent Shopify/HubSpot reports emphasize social discovery, short-form video, creators, UGC, reviews and conversational engagement.

Useful sources:

- https://www.shopify.com/enterprise/blog/social-commerce-trends
- https://blog.hubspot.com/marketing/social-media-reshaping-retail
- https://blog.hubspot.com/marketing/social-media-future

Questions:

- Which platforms actually matter to this audience in this geography?
- Is creator content followed by independent validation/reviews?
- Can social content naturally demonstrate the service rather than feeling like a hard sell?

## Local-market discipline

For Bolivia or another target geography:

- search local competitors and marketplaces
- check local payment expectations and major payment rails when relevant
- inspect local social channels and language
- consider purchasing power and price sensitivity
- avoid assuming US/European trust patterns transfer directly
- distinguish legal/official availability from informal market availability

When local public data is sparse, say so and use a small experiment rather than pretending certainty.

## Research output format

For a current market scan, produce:

1. **Question and scope**
2. **What changed / what is true now**
3. **Competitor/alternative observations**
4. **Customer/channel signals**
5. **Implications for The French Store**
6. **Opportunities ranked by confidence and impact**
7. **What we still do not know**
8. **Cheapest validation step**

Always attach dates to time-sensitive conclusions.
