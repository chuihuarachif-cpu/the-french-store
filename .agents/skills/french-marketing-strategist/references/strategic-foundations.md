# Strategic foundations

This reference distills useful operating principles from established marketing/growth work. It is not a personality imitation and should not be used to copy anyone's voice or proprietary material.

## April Dunford — positioning before messaging

Useful source material:

- https://www.aprildunford.com/post/a-quickstart-guide-to-positioning
- https://www.aprildunford.com/post/an-introduction-to-positioning
- https://www.aprildunford.com/post/my-positioning-book-obviously-awesome-launches-today

Core operating sequence:

1. Competitive alternatives
2. Differentiated capabilities
3. Value enabled by those capabilities
4. Best-fit customers
5. Market category/context

Application rule: do not jump directly to copywriting, slogans or channel tactics when the underlying positioning is unclear.

### Diagnostic questions

- If the product disappeared tomorrow, what would the buyer do instead?
- What meaningful alternative is the buyer comparing us with?
- What can we credibly claim that matters to a specific segment?
- Which buyer cares most?
- In what category/context does our strength become obvious rather than needing a long explanation?

## Rory Sutherland — perceived value and behavioral economics

Useful source material:

- https://www.ted.com/talks/rory_sutherland_life_lessons_from_an_ad_man

Key lesson for the agent: not every improvement in customer value requires changing the underlying product. Context, framing, confidence, convenience, status, salience and reduced anxiety can materially change perceived value.

Use this to search for low-cost nonlinear improvements such as:

- clearer choice architecture
- reducing uncertainty at purchase
- better framing of a bundle
- a more memorable ritual or reward
- progress/status that customers can understand
- personalized reassurance
- convenience that competitors overlook
- making an invisible benefit visible

Ethical constraint: never use this thinking to justify deception, fake scarcity, fabricated social proof or confusing price presentation.

## Reforge / Brian Balfour and collaborators — growth loops

Useful source material:

- https://www.reforge.com/blog/growth-loops
- https://www.reforge.com/courses/growth-foundations
- https://www.reforge.com/blog/the-growth-competency-model

The useful shift is from a one-way acquisition funnel to a system where outputs can become future inputs.

### Loop template

1. New/existing user takes an action.
2. That action creates an output.
3. The output is exposed to the same or another user.
4. A percentage of exposure creates another action.
5. The cycle repeats.

### Questions

- What is the loop's input?
- What user motivation drives the action?
- What is produced?
- Who sees the output?
- What conversion creates the next cycle?
- How long is the cycle time?
- Where does quality decay?
- Does the loop actually compound, or is it just a campaign with a circular diagram?

## Retention principle

Acquisition cannot permanently compensate for poor repeat value. Before recommending aggressive growth spending, inspect whether customers have a reason to return.

Retention can come from:

- recurring need
- superior convenience
- accumulated value/status
- saved information or preferences
- trusted service
- breadth/cross-sell
- community/social utility
- reseller economics
- habit

Do not manufacture artificial lock-in if the underlying value is weak.

## Practical synthesis for The French Store

A strong proposal should generally answer all four:

1. **Positioning:** why this store/offer rather than the alternative?
2. **Behavior:** why will the customer notice, trust and act?
3. **Economics:** does the mechanic protect contribution/margin and operational capacity?
4. **Loop:** if successful, can it make future growth or retention easier?

If an idea is brilliant on only one dimension, improve it before recommending full implementation.
